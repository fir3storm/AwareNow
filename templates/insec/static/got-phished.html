10 things to do (and not do)

  1. Do not type your real password, OTP, or PIN on a page you reached from an unexpected email.
  2. Do check the sender address, not only the display name. Look-alike domains are common.
  3. Do hover a link before you click. If the URL looks odd, do not open it.
  4. Do not send money, gift cards, or crypto because an email said it was urgent.
  5. Do report the message to IT / security. Reporting a click is better than hiding it.
  6. Do not share one-time codes from SMS or authenticator apps with anyone who emailed or called you.
  7. Do open the real website yourself (bookmark or typed address), then sign in there if you must check an account.
  8. Do not open unexpected attachments, even if they look like invoices or HR files.
  9. Do call a known IT number if you are unsure. Do not call a number printed only in the suspicious email.
  10. Do turn on MFA and change your password from a trusted page if you already entered credentials.
